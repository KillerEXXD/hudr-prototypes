ClubR — How the Games Work

ClubR is a scorekeeper for a poker club's side-games — it tracks and proves the count and the result, but never touches the cash (the club settles up offline). Before you test a game, read its section here so you know what should happen. Never played fantasy or poker pools? That's exactly who this is for.

Start here — the 3 games at a glance

ClubR runs three side-games for poker clubs. None of them is "playing poker" inside the app — the app is the referee + scoreboard. Here's the one-line version of each, then read the full section before testing it.

🎯 FT Fantasy (Stack Draft)
Fantasy poker. You draft 4 of the 9 players at a tournament's final table, then score points based on where they finish. Highest total wins.
🏟️ Last Longer
A survival pool on your club's own game night. Everyone buys in; whoever busts out last wins. The app keeps the live count public so nobody can hide players.
🏈 Football Squares
A 10×10 grid party game. You claim squares; secret 0–9 digits are assigned to rows/columns; the teams' scores pick the winning square each quarter.

🔑 Words you'll see in every game

Buy-in
What each player pays to enter (e.g. 100). ClubR only records it — money is settled offline.
Pool
Total prize = buy-in × players. Paid out to the winners.
Host
The club person who runs the game: admits players, marks who paid, enters results.
Paid (green toggle)
Whether a player has settled their buy-in with the host. Tracking only — no payment happens in-app.
Payouts (e.g. 50/30/20)
How the pool splits — here, 1st gets 50%, 2nd 30%, 3rd 20%.
Stakes / coins
The in-app unit shown on amounts. It's a scorekeeping number, not real currency.

🎯 FT Fantasy

Stack Draft
What is this game?

Fantasy sports — but for a poker final table.

A big poker tournament ends with a final table: the last 9 players left, playing for the title. In FT Fantasy you don't play poker — you act like a fantasy-football manager: you "draft" (pick) 4 of those 9 players before the final table plays out. Then you score points based on where each of your 4 players finishes (1st place is worth the most, 9th the least). Whoever's 4 picks score the most points wins the prize pool.

Analogy: It's like a fantasy football draft, except your "team" is 4 poker pros and their "stats" are simply how high they finish at the table.

The twist that makes it a game of skill — the budget & ICM

Chip stack
How many chips a finalist has. More chips = more likely to finish high. Shown like "35k · 95 BB" (big blinds).
ICM price
Each finalist has a price set by their chip stack (ICM = a standard poker formula for "what a stack is worth"). Chip leaders cost the most.
Budget
You get a fixed budget (e.g. 100k) and must fit 4 players inside it — so you can't just grab the 4 biggest stacks. You balance favourites with cheaper value picks.
Lock
Drafting closes at the lock time. Until then you can change your 4. Picks are sealed — the server can't read them before lock, so nobody can copy or cheat.
Finishing order
After the table plays out, the host enters the public 1st→9th result. This is known/streamed, so it can't be faked.
Finish points
Each place is worth points: 1st 100 · 2nd 70 · 3rd 50 · 4th 35 · 5th 25 · 6th 18 · 7th 12 · 8th 6 · 9th 3. Your score = your 4 players' points added up.
1

Find a contest & get in

On Games → FT Fantasy, open a contest that's Open. As a player you request to enter and the host admits you; you'll show as "Entered".
Expect: the contest lists buy-in, pool, lock time, payouts, and how many players have entered.
2

See the final table — 9 players, A–I

Each finalist (seats A–I) shows their chip stack and ICM price. Bigger stack = pricier.
Expect: exactly 9 finalists, each with a country flag, chip count, and big-blind count.
3

Draft 4 — stay under budget

Tap players to draft them. The header tracks budget left and 4/4 drafted. You must pick exactly 4 and stay within budget; over-budget is blocked.
Expect: "Budget 9k / 100k left · 4/4 drafted" updates as you pick; a 5th pick or going over budget is rejected.
4

Lock — picks seal

At the lock time the draft closes. You can change picks before lock; after lock they're frozen and revealed.
Expect: after lock you can no longer edit your draft; the contest shows Locked.
5

Host enters the finishing order → scores compute

Once the real table finishes, the host enters who came 1st→9th. ClubR converts that to finish points and adds up each entrant's 4 picks.
Expect: your score = sum of your 4 players' finish points; the highest total wins the pool (split by payouts). E.g. "You finished 1st · 223 pts".
Why ClubR exists here

The finishing order is public and the picks were sealed before lock — so the result can't be rigged and nobody can copy a winning draft. ClubR just proves it.

🏟️ Last Longer

Survival pool
What is this game?

"Whoever lasts longest wins."

On the club's own poker night, players add a small side-bet on top of the real game: everyone pays a buy-in into one pool, and the player who survives the longest — i.e. busts out LAST — wins it. It has nothing to do with who wins the actual tournament; it's purely about survival. The app is the live, public scoreboard of who's still in.

Analogy: Last one standing. Like a "who can stay in the pool the longest" bet among friends — ClubR just keeps an honest, public count so nobody can fudge it.

Chip count
How many chips a player has right now. The board sorts by this — chip leader on top. Updated through the night.
Bust / eliminated
When a player runs out of chips they're "busted" (out). The host marks them out; they drop to the bottom with a bust time.
Admit
The host vets and admits each player onto the board (and marks them paid).
Chop
If the remaining players agree, they can split the pool instead of playing it out — a "chop". Needs everyone's agreement.
The count
The headcount of who's in vs out. ClubR makes it public & un-hideable — the whole point.
1

Host opens the game; players join

Host sets buy-in & payouts. Players request to join; the host Admits them (green dot once paid). Admitted players appear on the board.
Expect: a waiting player shows an Admit button; after admit they appear in the leaderboard.
2

The live board — sorted by chips

Everyone still in is listed, chip leader at the top. Each row shows chips, paid status, and last-updated time. You can update your own chip count.
Expect: the list re-sorts when chip counts change; the "· N in · M out" header matches the board.
3

Update chips through the night

As stacks change, players (or the host) update chip counts. The leaderboard re-orders live.
Expect: entering a new chip count and tapping Update moves the player up/down the board.
4

Bust players as they're knocked out

When a player is eliminated, the host taps Out (or the player busts themselves). They move to the bottom, struck through, with a bust time.
Expect: busted players leave the active list and appear at the bottom as "busted Xm ago"; the "out" count goes up.
5

Last one standing — or a chop

Play continues until one player remains (they win), or the remaining players propose a chop and all agree to split the pool.
Expect: a chop needs unanimous agreement; the winner / chop result and payouts (e.g. 50/30/20 for the last 3) are shown.
Why ClubR exists here

Run on paper/WhatsApp, a dishonest host could hide players and pocket the early-busted buy-ins. ClubR makes the count public and un-hideable — players see the true headcount the whole time.

🏈 Football Squares

Grid game
What is this game?

The classic Super Bowl grid — squares win by the score.

A 10×10 grid (100 squares) for a football game between two teams. Players claim squares before kickoff. Once the grid is full, each row and each column is secretly assigned a digit 0–9. At the end of each scoring period (Q1, Q2, Q3, Final), you take the last digit of each team's score — those two digits point to one square on the grid, and that square's owner wins that period's payout.

Analogy: A raffle that's decided by the scoreboard. You don't pick numbers — you pick squares; the game's score draws the "winning ticket" each quarter.

The grid (10×10)
100 squares. One team's digits run along the top (columns), the other down the side (rows).
Claim
Tap an empty square to claim it (costs one buy-in). You can claim several. Your claim is pending until the host approves it.
Host approval
The host taps a pending (amber) square to approve it — then it's locked in to that player.
Hidden digits (provably fair)
The 0–9 digits stay "?" hidden until the host locks the board. They're assigned by a provably-fair shuffle — nobody (not even the host) can rig which square gets which digits, and anyone can verify it later.
Periods & payouts
Each period pays a share, e.g. Q1 10% · Q2 10% · Q3 10% · Final 70%.
Winning cell
At a period's end: side digit = one team's last score digit, top digit = the other's → the square where they cross wins.
1

Host opens a board for a matchup

Host creates a Squares game for a game (e.g. Chiefs vs Eagles), sets buy-in and the period payouts. Claiming opens.
Expect: a 10×10 grid with both teams labelled (one on the top axis, one on the side), and "N/100 squares" claimed.
2

Players claim squares

Tap empty squares to claim. Each claim shows your initials and is pending (amber). You owe buy-in × squares claimed.
Expect: tapping an empty square marks it pending with your initials; "owed" total = squares × buy-in.
3

Host approves claims

The host taps each pending square (or "approve all") to lock claims in. An approved square belongs to that player.
Expect: approving a pending square turns it locked/owned; a claimed square can't be taken by someone else.
4

Lock — the secret digits reveal

When claiming closes, the host locks the board. Only then do the 0–9 digits appear on the rows & columns (assigned by the provably-fair shuffle).
Expect: the "?" headers become digits 0–9 only after lock; the assignment is fixed and verifiable.
5

Enter scores → the winning square lights up

At each period's end the host enters the score. ClubR takes each team's last digit, finds the crossing square, and that owner wins the period's payout.
Expect: e.g. score 17–24 → side digit 7, top digit 4 → the (7,4) square's owner wins that period's % of the pool.
Why ClubR exists here

The digit assignment is provably fair (a committed random seed → deterministic shuffle), so it can't be rigged, and the grid/claims are public. The scoreboard decides — not the host.

✅ Quick test checklist

Only start testing a game once the section above makes sense. Then sanity-check these per game.

🎯 FT Fantasy

  • Final table shows exactly 9 players with stacks & prices
  • Can draft 4 within budget; over-budget / 5th pick is blocked
  • Draft is editable before lock, frozen after
  • Score = sum of your 4 players' finish points
  • Highest total wins; pool splits by payouts

🏟️ Last Longer

  • Host can admit waiting players (paid = green)
  • Board sorts by chips; updates re-order it
  • Busting moves a player to the bottom with a time
  • "in / out" counts match the board
  • Chop needs unanimous agreement

🏈 Football Squares

  • Claiming a square marks it pending with your initials
  • Host approval locks the claim in
  • Digits stay "?" until lock, then reveal
  • Score's last digits pick the winning square
  • Period payouts (e.g. 10/10/10/70) add up to 100%
